# Cameco Q3 2026

[**Replay Webcast**](https://event.choruscall.com/mediaframe/webcast.html?webcastid=OtqtVS49)

### Third Quarter Highlights:

##### Financial Highlights

- **Consolidated performance:** Third quarter results included net earnings of $43 million, adjusted net loss of $2 million, and adjusted EBITDA of $267 million while results for the first nine months of the year included net earnings of $199 million, adjusted net earnings of $279 million and adjusted EBITDA of $1.2 billion. Results for the quarter and first nine months of the year were generally lower than in 2025, primarily due to lower equity earnings from our investment in Westinghouse, as well as lower sales volumes and higher average unit cost of sales, partially offset by higher average realized prices. See *Consolidated financial results* and *Financial results by segment – Uranium* in the third quarter MD&A for more information.
- **Strong balance sheet:** Thanks to our risk-managed financial discipline, our balance sheet remains strong. As of September 30, 2026, we had $860 million in cash and cash equivalents, $1.0 billion in total debt and a $1.0 billion undrawn revolving credit facility.
	- **Dividend:** Our board of directors declared a 2026 annual dividend of $0.24 per common share, payable on December 15, 2026, to shareholders of record on November 30, 2026. The decision to declare an annual dividend is reviewed regularly by our board in the context of our cash flow, financial position, strategy and other relevant factors, including appropriate alignment with the cyclical nature of our earnings.
- **Uranium**: In our core uranium segment, third quarter earnings before taxes were $175 million and adjusted EBITDA was $179 million compared to $172 million and $220 million, respectively, in 2025, due to our normal quarterly variations in deliveries and our lower planned 2026 sales delivery volumes resulting from our contracting discipline. Earnings before taxes for the first nine months of the year were $703 million and adjusted EBITDA was $855 million, compared to $681 million and $862 million in 2025, respectively. See *Financial results by segment – Uranium* in our third quarter MD&A for more information.
- **Fuel Services**: In our Fuel Services segment, third quarter earnings before taxes were $13 million and adjusted EBITDA was $24 million, compared to $17 million and $25 million in 2025, respectively. Earnings before taxes for the first nine months of the year were $87 million while adjusted EBITDA was $120 million, compared to $129 million and $158 million in 2025, respectively, due to our normal variations in the mix of Fuel Services products sold. See *Financial results by segment – Fuel services* in our third quarter MD&A for more information.
- **Westinghouse**: Westinghouse reported a net loss of $24 million (our share) for the third quarter, an improvement from a net loss of $32 million (our share) in the third quarter of 2025. Over the first nine months of the year, Westinghouse reported a net loss of $80 million (our share), in comparison to net earnings of $32 million (our share) in the same period in 2025. Equity earnings from our investment in Westinghouse were lower than in 2025 due to Westinghouse’s participation in the construction project for two nuclear reactors at the Dukovany power plant in the Czech Republic, which resulted in an approximate US$170 million increase in our share of Westinghouse’s 2025 second quarter revenue. To better reflect the underlying operating performance, we use adjusted EBITDA as a performance measure for Westinghouse. In the third quarter of 2026, our share of Westinghouse’s adjusted EBITDA was $143 million, compared to $124 million in the third quarter of 2025, while adjusted EBITDA for the first nine months was $427 million, compared to $569 million in 2025. See *Financial results by segment - Westinghouse*, in our third quarter MD&A for more information.

Adjusted net earnings and adjusted EBITDA are non-IFRS measures.

**OPERATIONAL HIGHLIGHTS**

- **Uranium:** At the Key Lake mill, the annual maintenance shutdown began in the third quarter and included the installation of new infrastructure and repairs to major existing infrastructure. As a result of the substantial work, the shutdown was planned to be longer than in previous years and commissioning of the new infrastructure is underway. As we approach the end of the year, we have greater certainty in the timing of uranium production and deliveries. See *Outlook for 2026* and *Our operations* in our third quarter MD&A for more information.
- **JV Inkai:** JV Inkai remains on track to produce 10.4 million pounds of U <sub>3</sub> O <sub>8</sub> (100% basis) in 2026, of which our purchase allocation is expected to be 4.2 million pounds of U <sub>3</sub> O <sub>8</sub>. In the first nine months of the year, we purchased 0.8 million pounds of U <sub>3</sub> O <sub>8</sub> from JV Inkai and following the quarter, a shipment containing approximately 1.6 million pounds of our share of Inkai’s 2026 production was delivered to Blind River. The final shipment of our remaining share of JV Inkai’s 2026 production is currently in transit and expected to arrive at Blind River prior to the end of 2026. See *Our operations- Uranium 2026 Q3 Updates* in our third quarter MD&A for more information.
- **Fuel Services:** We continue to expect our annual production, which includes UF <sub>6</sub> conversion, UO <sub>2</sub> conversion and heavy water reactor fuel bundles, to be between 13 million and 14 million kgU. See *Our Operations - Fuel Services 2026 Q3 Updates* in our third quarter MD&A for more information.
- **Westinghouse:** In October, Westinghouse announced conditional purchase orders with four Polish suppliers to support the build out of the Polskie Elektrownie Jadrowe’s project and the first AP1000 unit at the Lubiatowo-Kopalino site. Separately, Westinghouse was authorized to begin procuring long-lead nuclear components, helping secure critical supply chain capacity, reduce project execution risk and support the planned development schedule. Also in October, Westinghouse launched a US State Department-funded FEED study for an AP1000 reactor at Slovakia's Jaslovské Bohunice site, advancing engineering, licensing and project planning for the proposed new nuclear plant, supporting the country's energy security objectives.

**MARKETING HIGHLIGHTS**

- **Deliveries and inventory:** In the third quarter, we delivered 5.6 million pounds of U <sub>3</sub> O <sub>8</sub>, which is consistent with our sales plan and reflects our normal quarterly delivery variations. With production totaling 3.5 million pounds (our share) and purchases of 1.8 million pounds of uranium (purchased at an average unit cost of $83.78 per pound (US$60.15 per pound)) in the third quarter, our uranium inventory was 8.5 million pounds on September 30, 2026, with an average inventory cost of $58.37 per pound. See *Financial results by segment – Uranium* in our third quarter MD&A for more information.
- **Contracting:** In our uranium segment, over the next five years, we have contracts in place for average annual deliveries of over 28 million pounds of U <sub>3</sub> O <sub>8</sub> per year, with commitments higher than the average in 2026 through 2028, and lower than the average in 2029 and 2030. As the market continues to improve, we expect to continue layering in volumes that capture greater future upside using market-related pricing mechanisms.

**2026 OUTLOOK UPDATE**

- **Production**: We have narrowed our guidance for our expected share of production of U <sub>3</sub> O <sub>8</sub> to be between 19.5 million and 20.5 million pounds (previously 19.5 million to 21.5 million pounds) for 2026, reflecting greater certainty in the timing of uranium production as we approach the end of the year.
- **Sales/delivery volumes**: We have narrowed our guidance for the sales/deliveries volumes in our uranium segment to 29.5 to 30.5 million pounds (previously 29 to 32 million pounds) as we have greater confidence in the timing of potential uranium deliveries as we approach the end of the year.
- **Financial metrics:** We have updated our guidance for average unit cost of sales, direct administration costs and capital expenditures. See *2026 Outlook* in our third quarter MD&A for more information**.**

**ADDITIONAL HIGHLIGHTS**

- **Westinghouse:** In September, the US government, through the Department of Commerce, and the Republic of Korea announced a non-binding framework that anticipates the investment by the Republic of Korea of up to US$120 billion to help finance the buildout of up to eight new large-scale reactors in the US, including six Westinghouse AP1000 reactors. The framework builds upon the 2025 strategic partnership between Cameco, Brookfield and the US government and is subject to definitive agreements and other conditions.
	- In our 2026 second quarter MD&A, we updated the business framework for Westinghouse, replacing the framework that was previously disclosed in our 2025 annual report. We have updated that business framework in our 2026 third quarter MD&A. See *Our Operations - Westinghouse* in our third quarter MD&A for more information
- **Changes to the executive team:** consistent with prudent succession planning and with Cameco’s ongoing commitment to execution of its balanced and disciplined strategy, effective January 1, 2027, the following changes will be made to the executive team:
	- Leah Hipperson will be appointed senior vice-president and chief financial officer, and
		- Heidi Shockey will assume the role of executive director, finance until March 31, 2027, at which time she plans to retire.

With these changes in the senior leadership team, we expect to continue to have the right people in the right positions, with the appropriate experience to help the company achieve its vision of powering a secure energy future.
